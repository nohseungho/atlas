Nepal Floods 2026: How to Help Now — and How Families Can Prepare for Flash Floods

Use verified official relief links for Nepal and keep that support completely separate from your own household preparedness. Do not ship unsolicited goods; follow official instructions and local emergency guidance.

1. Condolence / Human Impact

Our deepest condolences go to the families who have lost loved ones. We hope for the safety, recovery and support of everyone affected by the floods and landslides in Nepal.

2. Current Situation

Severe flash floods struck central Nepal on 26 August 2026. WHO Nepal says communities in Rasuwa, Nuwakot, Dhading and Chitwan districts in Bagmati Province, and Gorkha and Tanahun districts in Gandaki Province, have been affected. The Government of Nepal declared 15 municipalities in the affected districts disaster crisis-affected areas for three months.

UNICEF reported on 27 August 2026 that at least 17,000 children had been affected and launched a US$17.2 million appeal for emergency response and early recovery.

WHO reported on 30 August 2026, citing the latest report from national authorities, that over 730 people had been killed, nearly 2,500 were missing, more than 200 were injured, and 74 were receiving treatment. These figures can continue to change as search and assessment work continues, so readers should use the dated official sources below for the latest update.

WHO also reported on 30 August 2026 that about 10,000 households needed immediate relief. Four health facilities in Rasuwa were reported completely damaged, two in Dhading partially damaged, and hospitals in Dhading and Trishuli were inaccessible because of road damage.

4. What Affected Communities Need

The needs below come from the official event brief and the humanitarian organisations responding to the floods. They are presented as relief priorities, not as a shopping list for readers to ship independently.

  • Safe water and water purification to reduce risks from damaged or contaminated water systems.
  • Hygiene kits for households displaced or cut off from normal sanitation.
  • Emergency medical supplies and support for disrupted essential health services.
  • Medical tents where health facilities have been damaged or access is limited.
  • Newborn kits for families caring for infants during displacement.
  • Bed nets where post-flood conditions increase vector-related health risks.
  • Emergency shelter for families who cannot safely remain at home.
  • Food and nutrition support, including assistance for children at risk of acute malnutrition.
  • Psychosocial support for children and families dealing with loss, displacement and separation.
  • Emergency cash assistance so families can meet urgent needs locally.

Do not send personal goods to Nepal on your own initiative. Use official instructions only when a recognised relief organisation explicitly requests in-kind support for this event.

5. Prepare Your Own Household

The Nepal floods are a reminder that flash flooding can leave little time to protect documents, medicine, communication tools and basic supplies. Household preparation should be calm and practical, and it should never replace official evacuation instructions.

  1. Know your alerts. Identify the local warning system used where you live and know where official evacuation instructions will appear.
  2. Protect critical records. Keep copies of identification, insurance information, emergency contacts and essential medical information in a water-resistant location.
  3. Plan where to meet. Choose a household contact and a meeting point in case family members are separated.
  4. Plan for power loss. Keep lighting and a charged backup power option available for communications.
  5. Keep basic water and first-aid supplies. Store what is appropriate for your household and replace expired or damaged items.
  6. Think about medicines, children, older adults and pets. Add needs that are specific to the people and animals in your household.
  7. Leave when authorities tell you to leave. A go-bag is useful only if it supports a safe evacuation plan.
